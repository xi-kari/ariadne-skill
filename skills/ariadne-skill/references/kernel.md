# Lattice Kernel

## A method for advancing a question

The kernel organizes inquiry and work around a concrete difference: something to understand, a result to explain, an alternative to imagine, a choice to justify or an artifact to make. It does not start with a claim that everything is one kind of structure. Text interpretation, proof, experience, empirical research, design and normative reasoning retain their own standards (source §1.1–1.3, §1.10–1.12).

The operations and paths are Ariadne's derived engineering choices; their usefulness requires evaluation. A completed procedure or valid file does not certify truth, justice or real-world effectiveness.

## Hold the question and its representation together

State what is being asked, for whom, over which period and environment, with which allowed operations and acceptable error. Choose an object boundary and representation adequate for that question. A name or a complete object description does not establish a unified subject, a causal mechanism or a grouping gain (§2.1, §3.1, §4.17).

Ask three different sufficiency questions:

1. Can this representation answer the present query?
2. Can it support the proposed dynamic update?
3. Does it remain sufficient for the intended operation or intervention?

Passing one does not pass the others. Add the smallest useful distinction rather than trying to recover everything. A sum can answer a total perfectly while failing to identify an overloaded position. Information loss is task-relative; neither losslessness nor necessary loss is assumed (§4.18–4.19, Appendix C).

The task itself can be challenged. Reopen when its target, membership, loss definition, excluded alternatives or a supposed fixed constraint omits a material question. Preserve the old question and result, identify the proposer and reason, explain acceptance or rejection, and say whether the new question replaces, parallels or refines it. A new success does not repair an old failed test (§2.14).

## Match support to the claim

For each important claim, make these dimensions recoverable in prose or an appropriate artifact:

| Dimension | What to establish |
|---|---|
| Question and claim | What exactly is asserted or proposed? |
| Grounds | Which material, premises and inferential steps support it? |
| Identity | Observation, report, interpretation, formal result, mechanism inference, conditional projection, forecast, proposal, normative judgment or completion fact? |
| Scope | Which objects, task, time, environment and operations does it cover? |
| Dependencies | What unknown, disputed or failed item could change it? |
| Use | What does it support now, and what additional conditions would a different use require? |

These are independent responsibilities, not points on one certainty scale. A formal result may be proved within a model while its real-world mapping remains untested. An identified total effect may stand while its mediators remain unresolved. A completed draft may be useful while its adoption remains undecided (§2.1.2–2.4, §13.11.2).

Use clear statuses where helpful: supported within its stated scope, candidate, disputed, contradicted, not evaluated. A candidate is not confirmed because it is well formed or the only candidate found. Distinguish unknown, inapplicable, unobservable and withheld for protection; none automatically means absent (§2.9).

Keep sources, materials, observations, evidence, inference and external authority distinct. A source can be genuine while an interpretation of it is wrong. Several reports can share one underlying observation. Additional models, simulations, votes or citations do not create independent evidence. Record how measurements and inclusion rules produced the data; an improved indicator can reflect real change, narrow adaptation, selection or falsification, which require different evidence (§2.5–2.7).

Formal G/H-instance contracts apply when claiming their formal support. They are not an entry requirement for ordinary facts, established domain findings, text meaning, proofs or a person's ability to describe experience. Do not invent a pre-result registration for an existing study to make it eligible (§2.1, §4.1–4.8).

## Find the actual connection or break

Before connecting two results, identify what must carry the connection. Repair the actual failure instead of treating all failures as missing information (§1.11, Appendix C.12).

| Connection | Question | Possible repair |
|---|---|---|
| Representation | Does the next representation preserve the distinctions the final query needs? | Add a minimal distinction or explicitly change the task. |
| Causal response | Do the supported links operate together on the same cases under the needed conditions? | Study the joint response; do not multiply averages blindly. |
| Time and access | Was the necessary material or action available before the decision or deadline? | Change access, order, lead time or an authorized deadline. |
| Capacity | Can the actions coexist within shared resources and windows? | Reschedule, reduce demand or provide actual additional capacity. |
| Rule and execution | Did the actual rule correctly use the distinctions that were available? | Repair the rule or implementation without blaming inherent representation loss. |
| Authority | Does a valid authority cover this action, object and time? | Use the proper external process; precision does not manufacture permission. |

Representation composition, causal composition and joint resource feasibility need separate checks. A chain of locally successful steps can still fail; an independently established local result does not disappear with the chain failure.

Separate constituted status, participant understanding, actual realization and normative justification. A rule can create a right before service arrives; delivery alone does not establish legitimacy; a person understanding a rule is not the same as agreeing to it (§2.4.3).

## Explain, generate and test without collapsing them

Start with an adequate simple explanation or domain method, then add detail that answers a real residual, difference or task need. Compare reasonable competitors that the material permits and that could matter. If none is reasonable, report the search scope, why none was identified and what would defeat the current account. Do not create nominal alternatives to satisfy a quota (§4.8.4, §13.4 DS11).

Keep three history questions separate: how a state formed, how its current carrier preserves or transmits an effect, and whether history adds conditional information beyond the current representation. G3 concerns the last; a failed increment test does not erase historical formation or ordinary learning (§4.5).

Generation can change questions, representations, conditions, carriers, mechanisms, interfaces or decision rules. State the starting points, actual transformation, resulting candidate and appropriate check. A counterexample in a formal model may prove a limitation; a proposed real-world mechanism still needs evidence. “Not previously seen” is relative to the stated search, never a guarantee of global novelty (§4.18–4.19, §6.14, §11, §16.20.1).

Do not ask empirical testing to do every job. A proof needs valid premises and inference; a textual reading needs the relevant passages and context; a design needs fitness to constraints and meaningful checks; a normative recommendation needs explicit reasons, affected positions and defensible trade-offs. External effectiveness is a further question.

## Make judgments that the material supports

Take a position when grounds permit it. Say which interpretation is better, which proposed explanation fails, which design satisfies the constraints or which option is preferable under explicit premises. Preserve ties or genuine value disagreements where they remain. Avoid both invented certainty and a question list that withholds an answer already available (§1.11–1.12, §12.16).

Unknown states do not always prevent action analysis: in a finite declared model, an action acceptable in every compatible state can remain available without identifying the actual state. Check common feasibility including resources, protection and permission; individually feasible actions need not be jointly feasible. A formal intersection does not guarantee the list of real states is exhaustive (F3, Appendix C.12).

Finite choice distinguishes reasoning principles, substantive safeguards and a particular record format. Protection must not be weakened to improve an aggregate score, but this does not guarantee every outcome is unchanged. When every feasible option involves loss, give a reasoned recommendation under stated rules, retain unmet legitimate claims and continuing responsibilities, and reconsider artificial scarcity or excluded options. The recommendation does not decide for the authorized subject (§14 introduction, §14.4.1, §14.8–14.9.1).

## Carry results through delivery and revision

The user’s endpoint determines completion. Save and open the requested draft; test the relevant behavior of a code repair; inspect the changed external record; check actual arrival when the task is a remedy. Tool request, execution receipt, external state and downstream consequence are different facts (§13.11.2).

Change only dependent judgments when new material arrives. Preserve the original evidence cutoff, failed test, dissent and remaining local success. A reasoned unchanged conclusion is a legitimate result of review; merely changing a decision does not prove correction. Continue valuable work even when it leaves the headline unchanged (§2.9, §7.3.7, §14.5, §16.7).

The kernel is usable without a universal machine record. Use a durable state, ledger or model when it materially improves continuity and checking. Formal forecasts and consequential uses keep their specific responsibilities. See [Composition](composition.md), [Responsibility](responsibility.md) and [Continuity](continuity.md) for execution of these principles.
