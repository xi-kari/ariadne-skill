# Gates Contract

Sections 1-9 of the audit contract: subject freeze, transformations, main gates, expansions, strong-judgment items, formal tools, dynamic gates, the AI boundary, and intervention applications, plus the worked example. Governance, lifecycle, registries, challenge protection, capture detection, appeal and rollback testing, and retirement live in [governance-lifecycle.md](governance-lifecycle.md).

Use this reference for a substantive review, a formal gate register or an intervention audit. Keep the conclusions connected to the actual claims, evidence, affected positions and versions they concern. Section anchors (§) resolve through [source-navigation.json](source-navigation.json).

The tool layer turns the earlier contracts into observable, challengeable, reviewable, withdrawable bounded judgments; it adds no world facts, and form completeness never makes a fact hold (§13 opening). Within the source's formal diagnostic flow, the seven main gates carry the primary review; the five expansion gates and thirteen steps add detail rather than a second approval state. They are reusable checks within Ariadne, not a mandatory route through every ordinary task. **The tool layer never authorizes**: completed flows, evidence, and the strong-judgment eight yield only descriptions, candidate mechanisms, comparative judgments, strong-judgment candidates, or action requirements. Reading, checking original wording, and clarifying low-impact questions may use a proportionate short record, and not running the whole flow is never grounds for declaring existing domain facts nonexistent; formal diagnosis, forecasting, or high-impact use meets the full contract, and **renaming an output never lowers its responsibility**.

## 1. Freeze the Audit Subject

Separate four governance objects before judging framework health:

| Object | Audit question |
|---|---|
| Text and template | Do definitions, rules, forms, and versions contradict or hide obligations? |
| Concept relations | Do empirical, epistemic, human, normative, and operational claims cross their ceilings? |
| Actual practice | How do people and systems use the method in diagnosis, management, teaching, publication, and automation? |
| Governing community | Who controls naming, merging, interpretation, publication, review, and the costs of misuse? |

Record the subject type, exact version, owner, right holders, intended and prohibited uses, affected positions, observed harms, counterexample channel, falsifier, current lifecycle state, and current output ceiling. For a formal structural judgment, declare the relevant D0 object and scale; for a textual, formal or ordinary domain claim, preserve the corresponding object and proof duties; when the object, boundary, scale, window, K, or **use** changes, existing judgments are never carried over unchanged. Reopen the audit when the subject identity, evidence, scale, affected positions, use, or authority changes.

Never let a framework prove its own safety. Internal consistency, completed forms, validators, simulations, pressure tests, fluent reports, and community activity prove only that process artifacts exist.

## 2. Run the Four Transformations

Trace IT1-IT4 in the dependency order of the judgment under review. If a step fails, stop the downstream inferences that need it, preserve the break and state what remains justified. Do not withhold an independently grounded requirement or correction merely because another claim lacks observables.

| ID | Transformation | Required artifact | Failure ceiling |
|---|---|---|---|
| IT1 | Structural variable -> observable signal | Object, scale, observation position, time window, measurement protocol, and missing-state semantics. | Concept mapping or material-gap note. |
| IT2 | Observable signal -> evidence strength | Separate source, material, observation, comparison, coverage, counterexample, alternative, and uncertainty. | Ungraded material or testable hypothesis. |
| IT3 | Evidence strength -> judgment level | Claim contract, applicability boundary, reverse condition, falsifier, and withdrawal condition. | Candidate explanation or observation. |
| IT4 | Judgment level -> action requirements and ceiling | Affected positions, protection, prohibited acts, stop, appeal, rollback, and missing N/J/O/C12 conditions. | Description, evidence requirement, or inaction requirement. |

Failure semantics (§13.1): later steps that depend on the failed transformation are `not_run`, and the propositions, comparative judgments, strong-judgment candidates, or action requirements they supported stop or step down. **Downstream items sharing the same gap never recover their ceiling by renaming, splitting, or opening another branch.** Judgments with independent grounds whose use meets their own requirements are kept. A failure of a genuinely shared source identity, factual premise, object boundary or lawful-use condition stops or limits every output that depends on it. **When a high-impact adoption gate fails, the same judgment is not kept for its original use by relabeling it ordinary opinion.** Do not use a complete SJ package to bridge a failed transformation. Passing all four transformations still produces no authorization.

## 3. Run the Seven Main Gates

For a formal diagnostic or consequential-use review, apply IG1-IG7 in the order of the specific proposition and its dependencies (§13.3). Keep passed, failed, not run and reasoned non-applicability distinct. A failed prerequisite blocks its dependent pass; do not erase independently established evidence, protections or requirements. When object, evidence, affected positions or use changes, reopen from the earliest affected responsibility.

| ID | Gate | Pass question | Failure response |
|---|---|---|---|
| IG1 | Valid object | Do boundary, nine-axis scale, time, identity criterion, and use support this judgment? | Redefine the object, mark scale unknown, or review object conversion. |
| IG2 | Evidence trace | Can every source, material, observation, evidence item, counterexample, case, and judgment be traced without level collapse? | Return material organization, candidate question, or hypothesis. |
| IG3 | Affected positions | Are all direct, known or pointed-out low-power, and domain-required positions listed, with a proportionate, challengeable search of further effects (§14.4.1)? | Complete the material gap; pause only the dependent strong judgment or adoption, retain other unknowns with monitoring. |
| IG4 | Power and anti-retaliation | Can low-power positions safely add evidence, rebut, refuse, stop, appeal, and exit? | Make no adverse inference from silence; lower publication and judgment levels. |
| IG5 | Mediation and reflexivity | Do metrics, platforms, AI, publicity, or diagnosis change the evidence or object? | Represent a conditional state transition and stop uncontrolled recursive observation for every audited object, including the Stage-6 operation and observation-recursion cap in section 13 for confirmed S6 collectives. |
| IG6 | Strong-judgment procedure | Does every proposition that its use and foreseeable impact make a strong judgment submit SJ1-SJ8 in full? Public dissemination raises scrutiny but does not alone make every output strong. | The strong judgment and its high-impact use stop; keep an internal hypothesis, open assertion, or candidate mechanism; independently grounded comparative judgments whose use complies are reviewed separately. |
| IG7 | Action ceiling | What is the narrowest requirement, which acts are prohibited, and how do stop, appeal, and rollback work? | Description, gap list, inaction, or continued external deliberation. |

**IG6 applicability:** if the actual and foreseeable use does not trigger a strong judgment, state why IG6 does not apply; the remaining relevant checks can still proceed. If it does trigger, examine all eight SJ responsibilities and do not call IG6 passed with an absent or ineffective item. A checked string or full template is not evidence of protection.

Passing all seven gates means only that the record doors are complete. It does not prove a claim, select a value, or authorize action; IG7 records whether external normative and authorization layers exist and is never written as "authorized".

For IG3, distinguish a grounded unknown that could change protection or option comparison from an unknown that needs monitoring and reopening. Record searched channels, consulted positions, the stopping boundary and its reasons. The beneficiary of a narrow boundary cannot settle it alone; high-impact or conflicted cases need independent review. Newly discovered positions retain equal appeal, correction and remedy. No action and existing arrangements receive the same scrutiny, and remote uncertainty does not delay protection against known harm. If a challenge concerns the original target, membership, loss definition or supposedly fixed constraint, use the question-reopening procedure (§2.14) while retaining the earlier result.

## 4. Expand With Five Gates and Thirteen Steps

Use FG1-FG5 when complexity, responsibility, evidence collection, or reflexivity needs expansion. Feed each result back to the indicated IG gate; never issue a second approval state.

| ID | Expansion gate | Material | Main-gate destination |
|---|---|---|---|
| FG1 | Object | Object, boundary, scale, time. | IG1 |
| FG2 | Evidence | Signal cost, source, weight, and review. | IG2 |
| FG3 | Scale | Nine axes, within-scale object and evidence, and each scale change. | IG1, IG2 |
| FG4 | Responsibility | Rights and resource impacts, reverse conditions, repair window, appeal, and review. | IG3, IG4, IG6 |
| FG5 | Observation | Pre-observation baseline, reaction during observation, post-publication reaction, and a recursion cap on every audited object, including its Stage-6 instantiation in section 13 for confirmed S6 collectives. | IG5 |

Run DS01-DS13 in order:

| ID | Step | Minimum artifact |
|---|---|---|
| DS01 | Problem entry | Plain-language problem, requester, and intended use. |
| DS02 | Object and boundary | Effective object, subsystem, parent environment, and external channels. |
| DS03 | Signal layers | Separate facts, behavior, resources, feedback, narratives, feelings, conjecture, and noise. |
| DS04 | Observation participation | Awareness, observer power, publication effects, and candidate reactions. |
| DS05 | Dominant constraint | Candidate constraints, selected constraint, evidence, and competitors. |
| DS06 | State and window | Applicable state prototype reference, within-state positions, parallel subsystems, and reverse conditions; when S0-S6 do not apply, state why and keep the domain's own states and windows. |
| DS07 | Core structure | Anchors, carrying layers, transmission, boundaries, and feedback writeback. |
| DS08 | Protected variables | Protected states, damage path, resources, redundancy, repair, and return path. |
| DS09 | Carrying and repayment | Cost bearers, return recipients, stop rights, long-term depletion, and burden transfer. |
| DS10 | Hazard and boundary type | Maintenance debt, clock mismatch, proxy substitution, summary loss, and object-conversion candidate. |
| DS11 | Competing mechanisms | Competing mechanisms the material allows and that could change the judgment, required evidence, weakening evidence, and elimination rules; with no reasonable competitor, the search range and failure conditions instead of an invented mechanism. |
| DS12 | Branch outlook | Triggers, early signals, reverse conditions, repair windows, and minimum requirements. |
| DS13 | Output and writeback | Plain language, revision triggers, record scope, review, withdrawal, and rollback. |

Do not treat a human state prototype as maturity or fate. Let DS08 create protection requirements, not sacrifice duties. Let DS09 locate carrying and depletion without assigning complete responsibility. Keep DS11 within causal contracts, DS12 conditional, and DS13 out of organizational records, eligibility, allocation, and public memory until review and withdrawal are real.

## 5. Require the Strong-Judgment Eight

**A strong judgment is defined by use and foreseeable impact, not by tone, length, or confidence** (§13.5): a judgment intended, or reasonably foreseeable, to be used for handling, eligibility, rights, or resource allocation, or to materially affect a specific subject's reputation or public memory, and any judgment embedded in automated, organizational, or institutional decisions. Intermediate scores, recommendations, and material screening that feed those decisions are checked by their actual use. Public dissemination raises the scrutiny required, weighed with the evaluated subject, factual allegations, identifiability, reach, and consequences, but is not by itself sufficient to upgrade every output. Ordinary argument evaluation, mechanism explanation, and normative opinion that touch none of these uses may state clear conclusions with proportionate evidence and review. Calling an output commentary, research, or a draft never exempts it when it actually meets the condition. A formal strong-judgment candidate requires the applicable main gates and every SJ responsibility; even that status supplies no authority.

| ID | Component | Pass requirement |
|---|---|---|
| SJ1 | Reverse conditions | Specify what weakens, reverses, or withdraws the judgment. |
| SJ2 | Repair window | Define the window, evidence, and expiry. |
| SJ3 | Evidence requirements | Separate supporting, missing, and reverse evidence. |
| SJ4 | Appeal entry | Identify who can enter, owner, deadline, and how it can change the result. |
| SJ5 | Anti-retaliation | Protect covered conduct; monitor, independently report, and remedy retaliation. |
| SJ6 | Evidence supplementation | Allow new evidence, version correction, and downstream synchronization. |
| SJ7 | External-review trigger | Define trigger, independent reviewer, material access, and stop power. |
| SJ8 | Rollback and writeback | Name target state, repair owner, authoritative version, and synchronization path. |

If any item is missing, IG6 fails: the judgment does not enter the strong-judgment candidate set or keep its original high-impact use, and only an internal hypothesis, open assertion, or candidate mechanism remains; comparative judgments with independent grounds that neither depend on the missing item nor trigger the restricted use may be kept. SJ4-SJ8 verify at judgment level the same protections that PF-4 timed appeal, PF-5 anti-retaliation, PF-6 evidence correction, PF-7 external review, and PF-8 operational rollback bind at choice level; audit them as one list against the choice record's PF status instead of building a second registry, and treat SJ1-SJ3 - reverse conditions, repair window, and evidence requirements - as the audit-specific increment. If any item is absent or ineffective, fail IG6. A nominal appeal that cannot change a record or action is not an appeal. A reviewer under the same funding, evaluation, identity, promotion, control, or commercial chain is not independent merely because the label says so. A statement, apology, or new version is not rollback.

Responsibility increases with actual use and impact (§13.6), not with a universal level assigned to the answer. A low-impact explanation can give a firm, well-supported judgment; a high-impact use needs effective contestability, protection and correction even if its language is tentative. Limit the affected claim or use when evidence coverage, identity, reflexivity control, appeal or anti-retaliation deteriorates. Urgency never supplies authority or removes protection; identify the narrowest justified requirement under the authority that actually exists.

## 6. Use Formal Tools Without Circular Proof

This section carries the source numbering family `TOOL-*`: the evidence, assertion, claim, forecast, stress-test, and AI-boundary tools below are its shipped instantiation, and the family is resolved here rather than in a separate numbered registry.

Use these tools as bounded records:

| Tool | Proper use | Non-proof boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Evidence ledger | Separate source, material, observation, evidence, counterexample, coverage, and uncertainty. | A source does not automatically support a claim. |
| Open assertion | Create a challengeable, falsifiable, withdrawable target. | Do not use it for high-responsibility disposition. |
| Claim validation | Record claim type, support, counterevidence, falsifier, impact, and ceiling. | Completion does not prove the claim. |
| Forecast registry | Freeze target, window, baseline, signals, calibration, and result. | Keep failures and do not turn forecasts into fate. |
| Stress test | Find boundary, scale, and adversarial failure modes. | Internal wind-tunnel results are not external evidence. |
| AI boundary record | Disclose sources, gaps, responsibility, uncertainty, prohibited uses, review, and withdrawal. | AI cannot verify the world or itself. |

Allow tools to reference stable record IDs. Reject circular references that make the tools support one another without external evidence. Automatic checking suits closed fields, resolvable references, consistent version states, and reproducible calculations; it never replaces judging materials, affected people's testimony, causal identification, or value choice. Let programs carry the mechanically completable work so readers can examine the real reasons, and keep complete logs and clear prose separately but mutually locatable (§13.7).

Keep substantive pass, failure, non-execution and non-applicability distinguishable in the working artifact, along with unknown, unobservable and protectively withheld material. Apply a failed premise only to the claims and uses that depend on it. The audit itself never grants authority; when the host is already authorized to act, verify the requested endpoint and retain the evidence of actual execution separately from this review.

## 7. Add the Dynamic Nine When Needed

Use the relevant DF checks for dynamic derivation, a formal forecast, finite choice or intervention. DF6 applies to a forecast claim; DF7-DF8 apply to the corresponding recommendation or execution. A scenario need not register a forecast or invent an action merely to complete the list.

| ID | Check | Failure output |
|---|---|---|
| DF1 | Freeze event type, source, time, evidence cutoff, and dispute. | Fact-question list. |
| DF2 | Freeze actors, circles, relations, membership, and identity criterion. | Candidate grouping or simpler single-focus return. |
| DF3 | Separate material and experience-meaning channels; record five clocks and unknowns. | State snapshot without propagation. |
| DF4 | Register channels, thresholds, delays, feedback, and cascades. | Candidate mechanisms and differentiating observations. |
| DF5 | Register conditions, parent-child paths, early and reverse signals. | Parallel paths without forced ranking. |
| DF6 | Register target, horizon, simple baseline, calibration, and writeback. | Scenario only, not forecast. |
| DF7 | Register N, PF, affected positions, options, and inaction. | Pause only the recommendation the gap blocks; keep independent evaluations, conditional advice under explicit premises, and evidence needs; no execution permission. |
| DF8 | Verify J, O, responsibility, stop, rollback, and remedy. | External handoff, no execution. |
| DF9 | Append result, baseline comparison, error, downgrade, and retirement. | Preserve failure and open a new version. |

Keep every proposed circle or variable `candidate`. Do not present a scenario as a registered forecast when DF6 is unmet, or a recommendation as execution authority when its normative or authorization conditions fail. DF3 marks unrelated items not applicable with reasons and pauses only propagation that depends on a missing condition, reviewing independent paths separately. Never overwrite a DF9 failure.

## 8. Enforce the AI Boundary

AI may organize supplied material, align and check locatable sources, compare versions, find field gaps, generate and compare candidate questions and competing paths, check contract consistency, calculate registered metrics, and flag internal contradictions; **where evidence and explicit normative premises allow, it should also compare mechanisms, form refutable judgments, recommend options, and draft complete rules, plans, and commentary** (§13.8, §13.11).

Forbid AI from:

- inventing sources or treating training memory as current evidence;
- certifying real events, repair, safety, consent, anti-retaliation, or reviewer independence through its own generation, self-report, or internal flow;
- diagnosing personality or clinical conditions;
- disclosing for an affected person or treating silence as evidence or consent;
- deciding contested value premises, binding circle boundaries, decision subjects, or jurisdiction for others;
- converting a simulation into an observation or a forecast into authorization, or signing authorization;
- hiding uncertainty or independently making a high-risk real-world decision.

Every AI output states which materials it used, what is missing, which parts are observation, inference, or analogy, who bears final responsibility, how it may be used, and when it is reviewed or withdrawn; protective omission is distinguishable from "no evidence". Responsibility follows the actual division of labor among material providers, those who set goals and permissions, system maintainers, executors, and reviewers; facilities and programs do not become responsible subjects by outputting advice or executing instructions; more automation never pushes all responsibility onto the person who clicked last; reviewers need enough material, time, competence, and an effective power to stop. Fidelity to an original never proves that what the original says about the world is true. Variables AI proposes are recorded as model candidates with minimum tests and prohibited uses.

**Material checking** (§13.11.1). Check separately whether a source exists, whether cited information is correct, whether the original passage supports the sentence, and whether the world fact the sentence states has enough grounds. A given source not mentioning something does not make the sentence wrong; faithfully relaying a wrong source does not establish the fact. Local knowledge, oral accounts, and protected material without public web pages keep a verification path and disclosure scope. Retrieval augmentation and consistency checks are limited by retrieval coverage, shared sources, and error types: **stable repeated errors can look consistent, and several models repeating one false source are not several independent pieces of evidence**. A formal checker confirms a derivation, not its premises.

**Tool action chains and task endpoints** (§13.11.1-13.11.2). Record suggestion, tool request, application execution, external state, and consequence separately: a model writing "stopped" does not mean the executor received it, an interface saying "deleted" does not mean downstream copies vanished, and a human edit to a suggestion does not mean it took effect before an irreversible step. The verification endpoint comes from the user's request or the institutional task: a draft is delivered when correctly saved, openable, and meeting the requirements; changing an external record needs the external object's state, not just a tool's success return; actual remedy needs resources, rights, or services to arrive. **A narrower endpoint truly completed is acknowledged without waiting for every life consequence, and a wider endpoint is never replaced by a front-end success receipt.** Distinguish independently readable, independently modifiable, and jointly written objects; modifications to shared state keep order, version preconditions, and conflict handling; re-verify authority and stop conditions before any irreversible action. **A timed-out request means the result is unknown, not that it did not execute**: check by request ID or object state before retrying. Stopping is complete only when the stop was issued, the executor confirmed it, in-flight actions ended, and consequences already caused were remedied, each registered separately. A failed repair keeps the failed object and its real consequences and is never overwritten by a new normal output.

When auditing an AI-assisted governance artifact, check for original fact records, affected-position testimony, counterexamples, low-power feedback, external review, appeals that changed outcomes, evidence-control relations, and disclosure of model filtering or rewriting. If more than three of these categories are missing, cap the artifact at a lightweight observation or question list. Treat that rule as a conservative ceiling, not an empirically calibrated risk score.

## 9. Audit Intervention and Applications

Verify the chain `explanation -> diagnosis -> normative choice -> intervention`. For Ariadne's formal adoption record, require an externally existing choice, one selected action, all ten C12 components, explicit runtime N premises, O1-O4, all PF bindings, and one current atomic J authorization covering the same subject, object, action, territory, and entire period. Keep the Part 14 distinction between argument principles, substantive safeguards and this record format: a different implementation can carry equivalent protection, traceability and review after independent verification. A missing Ariadne field prevents its formal endorsement; missing substantive protection or authority prevents the dependent adoption. Neither finding erases independently authorized duties or already established domain knowledge.

Treat T0-T4 as task types rather than maturity ranks or required sequence:

- T0: establish a safe carrier for evidence, refusal, and help;
- T1: restore reversible minimum order;
- T2: repair a verified carrying-return chain;
- T3: maintain slow variables, succession, redundancy, and recovery;
- T4: exit, transfer, or preserve consent-bounded memory when repair is unsafe or unjustified.

For every T type, verify entry conditions, allowed and prohibited acts, evidence, C12, N, O1-O4, atomic J, PF-1-PF-10, ceiling, stop, appeal, rollback, failure, and rerouting. Preserve append-only execution states and keep `rolled_back`, `completed`, `expired`, and `withdrawn` terminal. For T4, also verify that any strategic-cost action is voluntary, informed, bearable in cost, explicitly recorded, and not exhausting of remaining strength; that exit is not romanticized as brave, loyal, mature, or the only right course; and that entry into a new carrier system re-registers from the new object's T0, scale, and identity criterion without carrying old authorizations over.

For a formal structural diagnosis or consequential intervention in a relationship, family, organization, platform, institution, public-opinion, space, emergency or ultra-large application, require the following. A topic alone does not activate this entire procedure for ordinary textual understanding, discussion or support:

- a D0 object, `SP=<A,X,T,O,C,R,I,N,J>`, identity criterion, time, and sources;
- all direct, known or pointed-out low-power, and domain-required weak-signal or unable-to-exit positions, plus further positions identified under the §14.4.1 boundary;
- the seven main gates and full protection floor;
- domain evidence coverage, source gaps, and the exact downgrade effect of each gap, chosen from the closed list in the application contracts' `common_rule` (never `none` or `ignore`), propagated claim by claim; partial coverage only lowers the ceiling and never voids independently grounded argument evaluation, procedural criticism, or conditional recommendations under explicit premises;
- a bounded diagnosis and action ceiling;
- a choice or explicit inaction;
- a candidate T type only;
- for real action, the full external choice, authorization, correction, and state-log chain.

For consequential execution, make the chosen action, C12 responsibilities, explicit normative premises, actual authority, stop, protected appeal, anti-retaliation, rollback and state history traceable. Verify that evidence and owners exist and that high-impact review is independent of the deciding chain. A substantively equivalent external procedure keeps its own format and authority; a missing machine key is not by itself a missing safeguard.

Treat a missing field as a reading aid only, never as a real-world disposition record. In emergencies, do not delay valid professional rescue authority; confine Ariadne to facts, risks, minimum protection, and later review unless an external rule grants a specific action.

Audit **protection versus outcomes** explicitly (§14.8): existing protections remain or strengthen, while losses in resources, opportunities or life outcomes require separate normative reasons. Treat a disputed classification as protection pending review. If all feasible options leave legitimate claims unmet (§14.9.1), assess the finite recommendation, stated decision rule and its proposer/adopter, retained loss bearers, continuing duties and reassessment. Verify that scarcity and excluded options are real and challengeable. A valid recommendation is not an adopted choice; unresolved nonwaivable-protection conflict never becomes Ariadne `authorized`, even when external lawful or emergency responsibilities require an accountable decision.

## Worked Example: AI Workforce Risk Score

Treat this as an illustrative audit, not a finding about a real employer or person.

The proposed score uses silence as a proxy for commitment, lacks a defensible observation rule and mixes person, role, team and model boundaries. These defects block the score's use for staffing or promotion; polishing the report cannot repair the missing premises. Trace whether any actual decision already relied on it instead of treating a tool receipt as proof of use.

Deliver a review with concrete repairs: withdraw the unsupported inference; retain verified workload or role facts independently; identify every score-derived decision and record; provide a reachable protected appeal with a response deadline; and assign independent review, downstream correction, restoration and residual-harm duties. If five of the source's malicious-compliance signals are actually evidenced, report the procedural high-risk trigger without inferring deliberate deception. The reviewer's conclusion is a supported rejection of this proposed use and a repair specification; authority to carry out each action remains with its qualified subject.

If the score is later withdrawn, append a child record that names the rollback target, corrected authoritative records, downstream synchronization, remedies, residual harms, and terminal lifecycle state. Never revive the withdrawn score by renaming it or issuing an internal approval.
