## Purpose and source binding

The Lattice Kernel is the common discipline behind every Ariadne module. It makes reasoning traceable without treating form completion as truth. Its core movement is:

> name the difference to explain -> declare task and representation together -> declare the candidate object -> freeze evidence -> audit scale, mechanism, and human positions -> derive paths -> register forecasts -> compare choices and, under explicit premises, recommend for deliberation -> govern use, correction, and writeback

Each arrow is a gate, not an automatic implication. A gate that fails stops only the judgments that depend on it.

Source binding: the kernel distills the method of *Xikari-条件结构与转化论* (source SHA-256 pinned in `source-navigation.json`). Every Ariadne entry is a derived method and retrieval entry with candidate status. It is not an author-confirmed item, does not add a second theory beside the source, and loses authority wherever it misreads the source text; when an entry and the source disagree, the source governs and the entry is corrected (source §16.20.1, §16.15).

## Fifteen invariants

### 1. Start from the difference, not the vocabulary

Begin with a concrete puzzle in plain words: which difference needs explaining, deciding, or protecting. Structure, psychology, interest, culture, power, or technology never earns final-cause status by name. A shorter adequate domain method is allowed to answer alone; Ariadne then contributes only source and boundary checks (source §1.3, §1.10, §16.15, Appendix D.01).

### 2. Declare task and representation together

Every object serves a declared task: target query, horizon, environment, allowed operations, and error tolerance. A representation that answers a static query may still fail to advance the dynamics or to evaluate an allowed intervention; check those three separately. Do not presume a translation is lossless, and do not presume it must lose task-relevant information (common method constraint 1, source §3.1, §3.4, §4.17.1).

### 3. Admit objects in two stages, for strong claims only

First declare a candidate object `S*=<B,X,R,I,T,SP,K>`; completeness only makes the question discussable. Formal support for grouping, channel, historical-increment, or closure claims requires a G-instance frozen before results. This two-stage gate governs strong framework claims; it is not a truth gate for ordinary facts, historical records, text meaning, or proofs, which keep their own standards (source §2.1, §2.1.1, §3.1).

### 4. Separate claim roles

Split any sentence that mixes roles: D definition, G empirical question family (instantiated only through G-instances), E epistemic constraint, H human-domain clause, N normative commitment, O operating procedure, and F conditional formal proposition proved inside a stated model. C contracts are result-use contracts and mechanism-condition combinations, not an eighth kind of fact; C12 is the normative procedure bridge (source §2.2, §4.10).

### 5. Separate dependency types

Only `inferential_requires` enters the inference graph. `protocol_requires` (evidence, causal, analogy, source, domain-classification gates), `specializes`, and `applies_to` constrain publication, scope, or applicability without becoming extra empirical causes (source §2.3).

### 6. Keep material identity and lineage

Reports, observations, model beliefs, formal proofs, simulation output, and execution receipts keep their own identity. Reprocessing the same material is not a new observation; recursion depth, sample count, file count, and repeated citation never add independent support (common method constraint 2, source §2.7.2, §4.17.2).

### 7. Use contracts for bridges

Causal, evidence, analogy, source, scale, circle-relation, and representation bridges each register what could break them. An incomplete bridge keeps a candidate relation and lowers the ceiling of what depends on it (source §2.4-§2.7, §3.4, §5.10).

### 8. Connect judgments only under named conditions

A judgment chains into another only when the conditions that carry it are stated: the same representation, the same cases, a joint response, the same resources, the same clock, the same rule, timely access, and valid permission. Locate the actual breakpoint and repair it there; keep the partial results that already stand (source §1.11, Appendix C.12).

### 9. Prefer the coarsest adequate representation

Merge states that the declared targets and allowed operations cannot distinguish; keep a distinction only when a task or operation needs it. Adding a circle, variable, mechanism, or layer must answer a named residual through a plausible channel and beat a simple baseline. Complexity is a cost to justify, never evidence of depth (source §4.19, §10.10, Appendix C.3 invariant 6).

### 10. Ask the three history questions separately

How earlier experience formed the current state, how a current carrier keeps and passes on effects, and whether history adds conditional information beyond the registered current representation are three questions with possibly different answers. G3 tests only the third (source §4.5).

### 11. Make forecasts fail visibly

Freeze target, deadline, object and circle scope, input cutoff, simple baseline, paths, expression, calibration, early and reverse signals, pause, retirement, and writeback before the outcome. A retrospective explanation is not a hit; a changed target is a new record (source §2.12, §12.2-§12.7).

### 12. Keep facts, values, and action qualification apart

Facts, predictions, formal feasibility, and technical capability never produce values, responsibility, or permission. An analytic recommendation is allowed when its normative premises, evidence, costs, affected positions, strongest objection, and change conditions are explicit; real adoption still needs protection, review, and valid authorization. The minimum action bridge is `descriptive or explanatory conclusion + explicit normative premises + current evidence + valid authorization + action ceiling + stop or withdrawal conditions` (common method constraint 3, source §2.2, §4.17.3, §12.8).

### 13. Downgrade proposition by proposition

A missing field, failed gate, or broken bridge lowers only the propositions that depend on it. Judgments with independent support keep their status; a failure of a global source, a shared premise, the object boundary, or the task's lawful use blocks every dependent output. Renaming, splitting, or opening a new branch never restores a lowered ceiling (source §2.9, §13.1, §13.3, §16.25).

### 14. Keep AI in the analysis layer

AI may organize and check material, compare mechanisms, form refutable judgments, recommend under stated premises, and draft complete plans or texts. It may not invent sources, treat training memory as current evidence, speak for a person's consent, read silence as consent, diagnose personality, replace a decision-maker, hide uncertainty, or make a high-risk real decision alone. Process products prove nothing about reality (source §13.8, §13.11, §16.9).

### 15. Govern and retire the framework

Internal consistency, validator passes, stress tests, and reports do not prove real safety or effectiveness. Preserve external evidence, affected-party challenge, failures, downgrades, alternative methods, version writeback, and orderly retirement, including for Ariadne itself (source §16.1, §16.21, §16.24).

## Output classes and the task chain

The source separates four outputs and four tasks. Ariadne's request types map onto them; each keeps its own evidence and stopping point.

| Request type | Source anchor | Question | Required stopping point |
|---|---|---|---|
| `explanation` | Output: explanation | Why did this happen? | Bounded mechanisms and named competitors; residuals kept; no prediction or action claim by default. |
| `diagnosis` | Task: diagnosis | Where is the bounded structural problem, and what would overturn that view? | Evidence-graded location of constraints, unstable links, and carrying gaps; finding a risk authorizes nobody to act. |
| `dynamic_derivation` | Output: derivation | Which paths can follow from stated states and events? | Branches, clocks, thresholds, signals, residuals, and stops. |
| `conditional_forecast` | Output: conditional forecast | Under a dated window, which outcome is better supported? | Pre-registered comparison against a simple baseline, then calibration and writeback. |
| `finite_choice` | Output: finite choice; task: normative choice | How do action, delay, probing, exit, maintaining, and no action compare? | Explicit norms, distribution, protection floor, and either an analytic recommendation for deliberation or a requirements handoff; never permission. |
| `intervention_review` | Task: intervention | Does a proposed action stay inside its authority, reversibility, and protection ceiling? | Requirements, prohibitions, stop, appeal, rollback, and the T0-T4 task type; only consumes existing authorization. |
| `governance_review` | Part 16 | Should a claim, process, model, or framework be restricted, paused, replaced, or retired? | Contestability, writeback, downgrade, replacement, retirement. |
| `synthesis_design` | AI drafting role (source §13.8, §16.9) | Which bounded design or plan fits the frozen constraints? | Constraint record, compared candidates including keep-current, and a bounded proposal returned to the host workflow; Ariadne never builds or executes. |

The fixed task chain is explanation -> diagnosis -> normative choice -> intervention. Each link adds conditions; none is crossed by vocabulary. An explanation can stand while prediction stays hard; a forecast can be accurate while an action stays unjustified; a recommendation can be worth deliberating while execution stays unauthorized (source §1.1, §1.4, §15.1).

Request-type adjudication: auditing how well existing evidence supports an already stated claim is explanation work at most at `diagnostic_only`, even when the claim is public. Governance review is reserved for restricting, withdrawing, downgrading, or retiring an already published claim, process, or model. "Do these two papers support this causal conclusion?" routes to explanation; "Should we retract our published conclusion after this counterevidence?" routes to governance review.

## Claim roles, dependency types, and kernel layers

| Role | Responsibility | Cannot establish by itself |
|---|---|---|
| D definition | Fix a term's local meaning | A real-world fact, mechanism, or historical effect |
| G empirical question family | Organize a testable question (G1 grouping gain, G2 specified channel, G3 historical conditional increment, G4 representation closure) | Support without a frozen instance; a law for all objects |
| E epistemic constraint | Limit observation, inference, and transfer; keep blind spots and residuals | A causal mechanism |
| H human-domain clause | H1, H4, H5 empirical hypotheses; H2 operating typology; H3 writeback classification; H6 normative boundary | A universal human essence; a rule for nonhuman worlds |
| N normative commitment | Declare what is protected or preferred | An empirical fact |
| O operating procedure | Freeze, audit, register norms, authorize, compare, limit execution, review, stop, withdraw, remedy | The truth or legitimacy of its input |
| F conditional formal proposition | State what follows inside an explicit model by proof | That a real object satisfies the model's premises |

The source arranges these into six responsibility layers. They state argumentative duty, not value rank:

| Layer | Answers | Authority boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Definition | What objects and terms refer to | Fixes decidable semantics; proves no real validity |
| Common method constraint (rules 1-3) | How to manage representation, material identity, and inference | Constrains records and use; not a law of the world |
| Conditional formal proposition F | What follows necessarily under explicit premises | Holds by proof; the real-world mapping needs separate evidence |
| Empirical question family G | Whether a specific empirical relation holds | Instances supply object, comparison, failure mode, and evidence |
| Result and mechanism contract C | What existing support may register and use | Valid only inside its dependencies, extra conditions, and window |
| Governance invariant | How to publish, evaluate, pause, and act | Limits power; never an empirical cause |

Dependency types:

| Relation | Meaning | Enters the inference graph |
|---|---|---|
| `inferential_requires` | Hard premise without which the conclusion does not follow | Yes |
| `protocol_requires` | Evidence, causal, analogy, source, or domain-classification gate | No |
| `specializes` | Narrows a general rule to a domain, object, or subtype | No |
| `applies_to` | Names the target of a norm or procedure | No |

## Evidence chain and bridge contracts

Keep these as separate records, each with its own conversion reason:

`source -> material -> observation record -> evidence -> counterevidence -> judgment -> action authorization`

A genuine source can carry a weak interpretation. Evidence can support association without causality. A causal conclusion can remain normatively silent. A value judgment can exist without jurisdiction or authorization. Absence and silence are not counterevidence by default; non-observation is not nonexistence (source §2.5).

Bridges register what could break them before any strong use:

- **Causal:** treatment version, outcome, population, window, scale, estimand, why the comparison identifies it, common causes, selection, attrition, measurement change, reverse effect, interference, competing channels, and failure conditions. Causal strength, mechanism resolution, and transfer range are graded separately; an identified total effect may stand while the mechanism stays open (source §2.4).
- **Analogy:** only a question generator; source and target domains, shared structure, differences, non-transferable elements, mapping, and discriminating tests (source §2.6).
- **Source:** domain, case type, time and place, support range, transfer boundary, counterexamples, and two-way links between judgment and original location (source §2.7).
- **Scale, circle relation, representation:** source and target, preserved, changed, collapsed, omitted, and new items, operator, error, validity window, and return path; the three transformation kinds are recorded separately (source §3.4, §5.10.1).

## Connecting judgments

Structural explanation is mostly about which judgments can connect, under which conditions (source §1.11):

- material that answers a total may not answer a distribution;
- two locally established causal edges may not be carried by the same cases;
- options that are each feasible may not share finite resources in the same period;
- valid authorization does not guarantee the action reaches its intended endpoint.

Name the breakpoint type before repairing: representation, identification, clock, capacity, rule, material access, or permission. A lost distinction needs the smallest added distinction; a misused rule needs rule repair; late material needs changed access or decision timing; missing resources or permission are not supplied by more explanatory precision. Keep earlier partial results that still stand.

Three composition boundaries never substitute for each other (source Appendix C.12, §5.7):

1. **Representation composition:** a chain of transformations still answers the same final query only if the final representation keeps every distinction that query needs (F4).
2. **Causal composition:** average local effects cannot be multiplied into a whole-chain effect without a shared joint response on the same cases (source §2.4.2).
3. **Resource composition:** actions feasible one at a time may be jointly infeasible when they share capacity, budget, staff, or a stop channel within one deadline (source §12.10.2).

A positive result on one of these is no evidence for the other two.

## History: formation, carrier, increment

Record cross-time claims on separate routes (source §4.5, §6.3, Appendix B):

| Route | Question | Minimum material | What it does not need |
|---|---|---|---|
| Formation | How did earlier inputs shape the current state? | Process or longitudinal material on the formation path | A G3-instance |
| Carrier transmission | Which current carrier keeps or updates the effect, and does it affect the target outcome later? | Measured current carrier, entry evidence, retention or update in the window, later effect, and identification for causal claims | A G3-instance |
| Historical increment | Given the registered current representation, does history still add information or effect? | A G3-instance (G3a predictive, G3b causal) frozen before results | Proof that formation or carrier routes failed |

When the current carrier fully represents what was learned, G3 can fail while formation and carrier transmission stand. Never omit a known current state to manufacture a historical increment, and never switch routes after results to rescue a claim. Learning, accumulation, persistence, delayed recovery, and reproduction earn support through their own contracts, not by name.

## Missing states and proposition-level downgrade

Use one of these labels instead of fluent completion:

- `unknown`: knowable in principle, unsupported now;
- `not_applicable`: no legitimate counterpart under this contract, with reason;
- `not_observable`: blocked by measurement, window, or channel; never rewritten as nonexistence;
- `withheld_for_protection`: material exists but disclosure would breach privacy, safety, or another named protection; record the basis, minimal scope, duration, and trusted review route.

Human-state classification adds `not_collected` and `conflicted`; see the human module. Protected withholding never raises confidence, and silence is not absence.

When a key variable is unknown, an inapplicability condition is unhandled, a core channel is unobservable, or protected withholding has no trusted review, lower the dependent propositions in order: candidate hypothesis -> question list -> description only. When authorization is unclear, harm is irreversible, appeal or stopping is unreachable, or professional competence is exceeded, pause real handling and hand off to the proper procedure. Register each downgrade per proposition with its dependency and the missing item's effect (source §2.9).

## Output ceilings and publication

The ceilings form the closed total order `description_only < diagnostic_only < requirements_only`. Each higher ceiling includes everything below it. Every level keeps `can_authorize = false` and `can_execute = false` (source §13.9).

| Ceiling | May produce | May not produce |
|---|---|---|
| `description_only` | Bounded facts and materials, questions, missing fields, candidate objects, and an unranked list of candidate mechanisms or hypotheses, each marked `candidate` | Mechanism ranking or endorsement, forecast, recommendation, action requirement |
| `diagnostic_only` | Description plus comparative mechanisms, bounded diagnosis, conditional paths, and eligible forecast evaluation | Normative requirements, recommendation, instruction, authorization |
| `requirements_only` | Description and diagnosis plus the conditions an external choice or authorization process would need and, when explicit normative premises, the protection floor, affected positions, and a no-action option are registered, an analytic recommendation for deliberation with reasons, costs, the strongest counterargument, and change conditions | Adoption, permission, instruction in imperative voice, execution, or any self-reported authorization |

Every root record starts at `description_only`; a higher ceiling is never an initial value. A child takes the minimum of the inherited ceiling and the module's local ceiling. A module run never raises the ceiling. A `lower` records its reason. A child may raise only after admitting new external evidence or recording fulfillment of a declared protocol, registered as a basis ID new relative to every parent; when the lineage was lowered before, state how the basis resolves that reason. A merge takes the minimum parent ceiling. More modules, votes, simulations, or confident prose never raise it.

Publication default (source §12.16): lead with the current judgment and its scope, then give the complete analysis that was actually performed: conditions, evidence, every substantive path comparison, each path's conditions, early and reverse signals, expression strength, pauses, and next review. Do not drop substantive analysis because it is a minor branch or does not change the headline; do not hide conditions in footnotes; do not let "the model thinks" replace evidential responsibility. Compress the visible answer only when the user asks for brevity, and keep the full analysis available for continuation.

| Publication state | Usable phrasing | Must avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Explanation only | "Current evidence supports mechanism A and keeps B open." | "So the next step will happen." |
| Derivation only | "If X persists and Y occurs, path P gains support." | "The model predicts P is settled." |
| Registered forecast | "Within T, P currently ranks above Q; see reverse signal S." | Hiding baseline, deadline, or failure condition. |
| Norms not passed | "Under the stated premises the analytic recommendation is P; real execution still needs N, PF, J, and O." | "Not yet authorized but ready to execute." |
| External authorization exists | "The authorization record lets that subject act within its ceiling and stop on these conditions." | Attributing the authorization to the model. |

## AI and self-governance boundary

Every run keeps `authorization_effect: none`. External choice, jurisdiction, consent, or authorization records can be linked and checked; Ariadne never creates them.

AI output states which materials were used, what is missing, which content is observation, inference, or analogy, who bears final judgment, the permitted use, and when it is reviewed or withdrawn. Protective omission stays distinguishable from "no evidence". Check a generated statement on four separate points: does the cited source exist, is the citation accurate, does the passage support the sentence, and is the world-fact itself adequately supported. Several models repeating one false source are one lineage, not several confirmations (source §13.8, §13.11.1).

For tool-using work, record suggestion, tool request, application execution, external state, and task consequence separately. A model writing "stopped" does not prove the executor received the stop; a timeout means the result is unknown, so check the request ID or object state before retrying. The verification endpoint is set by the user's request: a draft is done when it is saved, opens, and meets the request; a changed external record needs the external state confirmed; a remedy needs the resource, right, or service to arrive (source §13.11.2).

Apply the same discipline to Ariadne itself: keep source and interpretation separate, compare against simpler and professional methods on the same task and budget, record failures and harms, accept affected-party challenge, narrow claims after counterexamples, pause high-harm use, and replace or retire modules that do not add value. A user never needs to learn this framework before protecting themselves, recording facts, or seeking support (source §16.16).

## Peer unavailability clause

This is the single canonical text of the suite's downgrade clause; every SKILL.md states it as a one-line declaration and defers here:

> If a routed peer skill is unavailable in this installation, record the handoff in module_runs as not_run with reason module_unavailable, keep that responsibility's outputs unknown, preserve or lower the output ceiling, and do not execute the peer's responsibility inline or claim it ran.

The declaration form used by the modules is: `Peer unavailable -> not_run/module_unavailable, outputs unknown, ceiling preserve-or-lower, never inline (full clause: lattice-kernel.md, Peer unavailability clause).`

## Quick reference

Strong-judgment ten questions (source §1.6): Does the object hold? Is the evidence sufficient? Does the causal bridge exist? Was scale swapped silently? Were alternative explanations compared? Are rights harmed? Is the authorization valid? Can the action be stopped? Can consequences be remedied? Can the conclusion be appealed? When one cannot be answered, say whether it limits the fact judgment, the mechanism, the transfer range, the publication status, or the real action, and downgrade or stop accordingly. The questions are not a scorecard.

Protection floor (source §1.7): keep people's standing as subjects, a channel for dissent, a possibility of exit, minimal harm, traceable responsibility, and real rollback. Inability to exit, difficulty speaking, or unwillingness to disclose is never read as consent, absence of loss, or absence of objection. Holding more material or more confidence never widens the analyst's power to act.
