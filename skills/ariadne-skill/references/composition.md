# Composing work

## Build a path, not a procession

A path is a reasoned arrangement of work around a desired result. Start with the user's endpoint and the material already available. Determine which questions must be answered before another step is meaningful, which can be investigated in parallel, and which artifact will carry the result forward. Do not run every named operation, and do not treat the sequence below as a mandatory cycle.

The source supplies distinct outputs, task-relative representation, mechanism retrieval, conditional derivation, finite choice and correction (§1.1, §4.17–4.19, §6.14, §11–§14, §16). The recipes here are Ariadne's engineering combinations. A new combination can be useful without being an additional theory proposition.

## Reusable moves

| Move | Trigger | Do the work | Useful product |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reopen | The question excludes a person, loss, alternative or changeable constraint | Name the disputed given, consider the new question, explain its disposition, preserve the old result | A replacement, parallel or refined question with inheritance boundaries |
| Separate | One sentence mixes claims with different grounds | Distinguish status, meaning, effect, value, permission or completion; test each appropriately | Several bounded answers instead of one vague verdict |
| Trace | A claim rests on a source, metric or result | Recover provenance, measurement and inference; locate what each item actually supports | An evidence chain and precise gaps |
| Refine | One representation collapses task-relevant differences | Find a witness pair with the same representation and different required answers; add the minimal useful distinction | A better representation, algorithm or measurement |
| Locate the break | A chain has local successes but misses its endpoint | Test representation, same-case causality, clocks, access, rules, capacity and authority separately | A repair at the actual failing link |
| Explain | A difference needs an account | Compare a simple adequate account with reasonable alternatives; identify channel, conditions and defeating evidence | A supported explanation or discriminating observation |
| Substitute | A needed condition is unavailable or harmful | Replace a carrier, resource, interface, schedule or enabling arrangement; recompute dependencies and costs | A new conditional design |
| Recombine | Established or explicit relations might yield a new capability | Join them through declared interfaces; check representation, causal and resource composition separately | A candidate system with an inspectable derivation |
| Branch | An event, choice or condition could change | Change that node and its downstream consequences, preserve unaffected premises, identify early and reverse signals | Conditional paths or switching conditions |
| Compare | Alternatives support different results or burdens | Fix the question and relevant baseline; compare quality, cost, feasibility, distribution and protection under explicit criteria | A local ranking, tie, conditional recommendation or declared conflict |
| Challenge | A claim or design could fail in an important way | Build the strongest relevant counterexample or competing account; inspect sensitivity and excluded positions | A concrete objection and reasoned disposition |
| Realize | A useful candidate can become a requested artifact | Write, implement, arrange or repair within authorization; inspect the actual endpoint | A usable artifact and meaningful verification |
| Revise | New evidence, constraints or outcomes arrive | Identify changed dependencies, update affected results and current artifacts, retain the prior failure and stable findings | A complete revised result and the next live question |

Use professional operations directly where they are better: a theorem proof, a database query, a legal interpretation or a design critique need not be translated into structural vocabulary to count as work. The moves help choose and connect methods; they do not replace domain competence.

## From explanation to discovery

An explanation can do more than close a question. Look at what remains outside its scope and generate a new question from that precise limit. If training improved the measured skill but employment did not improve, preserve the learning result and investigate conversion conditions: availability of suitable work, recognition, access, timing or control. Do not rewrite the skill gain as nonexistent, or declare an unmeasured conversion mechanism established.

The resulting path can be: understand the two results → separate skill from opportunity → locate their connection → generate mechanism candidates → design an observation that distinguishes them. The final product may be a research question and a collection plan, without a recommendation to intervene (§4.5, §7.6, Appendix D.25).

## From a breakdown to a new capability

Treat a failure as information about a particular connection. Suppose an aggregate workload metric answers total demand accurately but cannot show which station is overloaded. Find the collapsed distinction, add only the needed position-level signal, and implement a corresponding alert. Then check whether the actual rule uses it, whether it arrives in time and whether response capacity exists. The signal repair does not alone repair staffing.

This combines understand → refine → create → review. A meaningful test includes both the old failure case and a case in which the old total is still sufficient. Preservation of the narrow old success is part of the repair (§5.10.2, Appendix C.7–C.9).

## From counterfactuals to a switching plan

Use a counterfactual to identify what would reverse a judgment. Change an explicit node rather than imagining a whole world without constraints. Propagate the changed information, opportunities, resources and later decisions; removing an announcement also removes the information that announcement supplied.

Search for a small decisive change only within a declared scope. “This is a sufficient reversal condition” is often supportable; “this is the globally smallest possible change” requires a complete search or proof. Turn the condition into a feasible observation, threshold review or alternative design. A switching plan also needs the practical lead time and authority for the switch (§11.9, §12.6).

## From constraints to invention

Before accepting “there is no alternative,” distinguish physical constraints, current resource allocations, negotiated deadlines, institutional rules and assumptions. Some are fixed within the task; others can be challenged through a legitimate process. Substitute or rearrange only what is actually available or explicitly hypothetical.

For two reviews competing for one time window, candidate changes might alter the schedule, reduce review demand or provide another qualified reviewer. Their enabling conditions differ. Merely renaming one window does not create capacity. A draft can explore an authorized deadline change while retaining the original schedule's infeasibility (§2.14, §6.10.1, Appendix C.12).

## From normative disagreement to parallel designs

If people disagree about a decision rule, more empirical precision may not settle the disagreement. Keep factual findings shared, make the rules explicit, and derive a design under each rule. Compare what each protects, sacrifices and leaves unresolved. Give a conditional recommendation when a rule is supplied; do not hide a binding value choice inside a technical score.

The path may combine discover → decide → create: uncover an omitted premise, generate designs that embody different priorities, then draft the rules and procedures that make the consequences inspectable. All feasible designs may involve loss. Preserve the affected positions' remaining claims rather than inventing a costless synthesis (§14.8–14.9.1).

## Admit work by dependency and value

Add a move when it can answer an unresolved question, establish a needed premise, uncover a meaningful alternative, clarify a condition, test a failure, protect an affected position or complete the requested result. A move can be valuable without changing the headline. Remove it when it adds only vocabulary or duplicate paperwork.

Parallelize independent evidence searches, candidate constructions or challenges when they can share the needed material without conflicting writes or resource commitments. A worker returns its actual result, grounds, scope, unresolved dependencies and produced files. Workers may use several moves; no worker must impersonate a fixed path or module. Repeated analysis of shared data is still one evidence lineage.

Merge conclusions according to their dependencies. Preserve a proof and an unresolved empirical mapping as separate results. Keep incompatible value premises or object boundaries visible. Do not average disagreement into consensus or apply the weakest lane's limit to unrelated findings. Use [continuity](continuity.md) to retain the actual material and versions needed for a sound handoff.
