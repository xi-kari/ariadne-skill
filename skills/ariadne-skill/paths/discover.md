# Discover: turn understanding into new possibilities

## When to use this path

Use this path when the user asks for a new angle, a stronger challenge, an unexamined question, an alternative mechanism or an inventive design. It also helps when a current explanation leaves a meaningful residual, an existing solution is inadequate, or a supposedly fixed condition deserves scrutiny. Do not confuse discovery with announcing a new fact.

Read the [kernel](../references/kernel.md) and use the relevant moves in [composition](../references/composition.md). This path combines task reopening, representation, conditional reasoning, mechanism retrieval and critique (§2.14, §4.17–4.19, §6.14, §11, §16.20.1). Its recipes are derived candidates, not a new theorem or a guarantee of novelty.

## Establish a launch point

Identify the current finding, model, artifact or question from which the exploration proceeds. Separate what is established, what follows inside an explicit model, what the user prefers and what is merely assumed. If necessary, do enough [understanding](understand.md) to avoid generating from a misunderstanding. Do not force a complete causal explanation before a useful design can be proposed under explicit assumptions.

State what is missing or unsatisfactory: an unexplained difference, an inaccessible option, a recurring failure, a hidden burden, an excessively narrow question or an opportunity for a different experience. Creation can be valuable for its own sake; it need not be justified as repairing a defect.

## Choose a productive transformation

Try the transformations that actually fit the problem:

- **Question change:** expose a contested objective, membership rule, loss definition or given constraint. Form a replacement, parallel or refined question while retaining the original result.
- **Counterexample:** find a case that satisfies the proposed premises but defeats the conclusion. Separate a formal counterexample from a real-world case still needing verification.
- **Representation change:** find which differences the current representation merges and which of them the task actually needs. Construct an alternative with the smallest adequate additions.
- **Condition substitution:** replace a carrier, resource, interface, sequence, deadline or enabling condition. State whether the replacement exists, is within authority to change, or is hypothetical.
- **Mechanism recombination:** connect two independently meaningful relations through a shared interface. Test representation, same-case causal and resource composition separately.
- **Role or position change:** examine how the problem appears from an excluded or burden-bearing position. Generate a question or design without pretending to know that person's preferences or consent.
- **Simplification:** remove a layer, metric or process and ask whether the task and protections still hold. Less machinery can be an inventive solution.

No transformation is mandatory, and no candidate quota applies. Generate far enough to expose materially different possibilities, then develop the ones that matter. Several renamed versions of the same idea are not substantive alternatives.

## Make the candidate inspectable

For each candidate developed, explain its starting points, the actual transformation, the resulting claim or proposal, its identity, what it competes with, and how it could be tested or defeated. Use prose, a sketch, an example or a prototype as appropriate. A label such as “new governance model” is not yet a candidate someone can evaluate.

Distinguish:

| Candidate identity | Appropriate next check |
|---|---|
| A new question | Does it expose a real omission or distinction, and how does it relate to the old question? |
| A formal conjecture | Check premises, construct counterexamples, prove it or state its unresolved status. |
| A mechanism candidate | Identify channel, conditions, observables, plausible competitors and discriminating material. |
| A design | Check feasibility, intended experience or function, trade-offs, affected positions and necessary authority. |
| A new measurement | Check what it distinguishes, how data would be produced and whether its burden is proportionate. |
| A creative artifact | Make enough of the work to assess its form and intent; empirical effects remain separate claims. |

Finding no prior instance within a search does not establish global originality. Internal simulation, repeated generation and model agreement are not new external evidence. A residual opens a question; it does not automatically discover a real hidden variable.

## Test, compare and continue

Challenge the candidate at its weakest necessary connection. Ask whether it changes the requested result, what important distinctions or protections it loses, whether its resources can be jointly committed, and what would make it fail. Compare a simple adequate alternative and the current arrangement when relevant. Keep positive findings that survive a broader failure.

Use [project](project.md) to derive conditional consequences, [decide](decide.md) to compare normative trade-offs and [create](create.md) to construct the actual artifact. Do not stop after “this could be interesting” when the request is to develop a usable proposal. If candidates cannot yet be ranked, deliver the developed alternatives and the specific discriminating check.

## Worked example: from waiting-time improvement to a new question

A synthetic service reduces average waiting among registered users, yet people unable to register remain excluded. Preserve the measured improvement for its actual population. Reopen the question from “how fast is service for registrants?” to a parallel question about reaching service at all.

Candidate designs could change entry requirements, provide a supported intake channel or measure the delay before registration. Their proposed mechanisms and costs differ. A new measure alone does not fix access; an assisted channel may depend on staff capacity and privacy safeguards. Develop the appropriate candidate, identify who it may still exclude, and propose a check that observes actual entry and service arrival. No claim that access already improved follows from the design.

## Completion

Deliver the substantive new questions or candidates, their derivation, the comparison or uncertainty, and the next test or artifact. Explain why they add something beyond restating the original answer. Preserve the old problem's result, including failure, and the permission boundary of any proposed real action.
