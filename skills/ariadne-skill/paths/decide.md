# Decide: recommend, design, and preserve legitimate claims

Use this path when the user needs an option comparison, a clear recommendation, an intervention design, or an institutional draft. Produce that result under explicit factual and normative premises. A reasoned finite recommendation is possible amid uncertainty, dissent, and unavoidable loss; it is not equivalent to permission to impose it.

Read [responsibility](../references/responsibility.md) and the [kernel](../references/kernel.md). The [choice and authorization reference](../references/library/choice-and-authorization.md) supplies the detailed normative contract; [intervention types](../references/library/intervention-levels.json) distinguish containment, repair, redesign, and exit tasks without ranking them as maturity stages. Use [human-domain reasoning](../references/library/human-domain.md) when people, roles, capacity, support, or power are central.

## Establish the decision, not a universal form

Identify what is being chosen, by whom, for which purpose, over what horizon, and who can bear the effects. Separate the empirical account, the values or obligations guiding comparison, and the actual authority to adopt or execute. Preserve verified existing permission; the method neither creates it nor automatically cancels it. Ordinary authorized editing, drafting, coding, or testing can continue through the host tools without becoming a governed collective choice.

Match the work to consequence. A personal low-impact preference may need a brief comparison and an answer. A decision affecting others' rights, work, resources, access, safety, or exit needs the substantive protections and contestability relevant to those effects. The source distinguishes argument principles, adoption safeguards, and a particular record format. Formats may vary; removing a protected position, shifting a cost or decider, or removing a stop or remedy is a substantive change. An independently reviewable equivalent procedure may satisfy the safeguards without reproducing Ariadne's fields (Part 14 introduction).

Do not treat the requester's initial objective or constraints as beyond question. If “maximize throughput” ignores an imposed burden, or “there is no alternative” depends on an actor's refusal to use its resources, reopen that premise. Retain the original comparison and explain why the new question replaces, refines, or accompanies it (§2.14).

## Build the comparison that can support a judgment

Compare materially feasible options, including a meaningful no-new-action baseline and relevant changes, delay, probing, maintenance, or exit. These are kinds to consider, not a quota to fabricate. No new action does not suspend continuing duties; maintaining the current arrangement may require active work and resources. Make the baseline's costs and burdens as visible as those of intervention (§12.9, §14.4).

For each serious option, trace the proposed change to a result under its conditions. Distinguish technical feasibility, usable capacity, timing, access, authority, consent, implementation resources, and expected consequences. A formally feasible action can be unavailable to this actor. An institution can confer a right while failing to deliver it. Explain what uncertainty changes the comparison and what leaves the same choice robust.

State the decision rule and why it is relevant: it may prioritize a right or duty, minimize a defined harm, use proportionality, preserve a viable minimum, or apply another justified domain rule. A minimax rule is not universally required. Do not quietly choose the user's values or aggregate away a protected position. When a material normative premise is disputed, compare the conditional recommendations under the competing premises and identify the genuine disagreement.

Make a recommendation when the comparison supports one. Explain the decisive reason, what it costs, the strongest remaining objection, and what would change it. If no recommendation is supported, name the precise missing fact, unresolved normative choice, protection failure, or authority issue; still deliver the viable comparison, conditional draft, or next discriminating step. Avoid an unexplained “insufficient information.”

## Trace affected positions proportionately

Include directly affected people, known or specifically raised low-power positions, and positions required by the domain. Trace other indirect effects in proportion to their gravity, reversibility, number, accumulation, and vulnerability. Continue until further inquiry could no longer reasonably change the comparison or protection assessment; exhaustive omniscience is not required. Keep the searched channels, consulted positions, unresolved boundaries, and reasons for excluding a raised channel open to challenge. Beneficiaries cannot unilaterally close the boundary; high-impact or conflicted cases need appropriate independent review (§14.4.1).

Distinguish unknowns that could defeat protection or change the decision from unknowns that can be monitored without doing so. The former block the dependent adoption unless inquiry, narrowing, or protection resolves them. The latter need a suitable follow-up. Later-identified affected people retain equal claims to remedy without proving that their harm was foreseeable. Inability to identify every indirect effect does not justify delaying protection against a known harm.

People need not reveal all private material to obtain protection or be heard. Use trusted or protected channels where necessary. Entry consent, current consent, practical exit, and ability to contest are different conditions. A participant's continued presence does not by itself show voluntary acceptance when the arrangement changed their alternatives. Having a response channel does not establish power to change an agenda, decide the matter, or obtain independent review (§14.5).

## Distinguish loss, dissent, and protection failure

Keeping the protection floor does not mean every outcome for everyone remains at least as good. Established protective arrangements cannot be silently redescribed as ordinary benefits to be traded away. When it is disputed whether an interest is a protection or an outcome, treat it as protection while the dispute is resolved; then compare ordinary outcomes under the stated normative rule (§14.8).

Dissent existing, a material reason remaining unanswered, and protection failing are three different states. A reasoned refusal can leave a decision unchanged when its grounds survive review. Do not require symbolic concessions to prove responsiveness. Conversely, acknowledgment alone is not a remedy when the objection identifies a wrong decision or an undelivered right (§14.5, §16.19).

When every feasible option imposes losses, first examine whether the scarcity is real, who controls it, whether earlier choices can be reopened, whether options costly to the decider were omitted, and whether an authority gap can be routed to someone competent. If losses remain unavoidable, give the best finite recommendation under a disclosed rule. Name its proposer and legitimate adopter, the loss bearers, legitimate claims left unmet, continuing duty owners, and the evidence, resources, authority, or date that can reopen the choice. Selecting an option does not extinguish the losing claims (§14.9.1).

If independently reviewed conditions show that nonwaivable protections cannot all be preserved, the method cannot grant an endorsement that waives them. It can still expose the conflict, compare limited alternatives, draft protective measures, and identify a lawful or emergency decision process with narrow scope, expiry, and review. The actual competent authority must supply its own basis; a method label supplies none.

## Make institutional design an actual artifact

Translate the comparison into a usable policy, protocol, agreement, allocation rule, or service design. Specify the target and scope, entry and current participation terms, actors and their powers, resource commitments, timing, implementation interface, and how people obtain what is promised. Give an accessible objection route, independent review when required, stop and correction triggers, and continuity or exit arrangements. Where a governing premise is unresolved, provide alternative clauses and the consequence of selecting each.

Test the draft against concrete cases: the least powerful affected position, an unavailable responsible actor, delayed resources, a reasonable dissenter, protected non-disclosure, a later-discovered harm, and an exit that transfers rather than resolves a duty. Select cases relevant to the design; do not demand a ritual checklist for a small draft. Correct the artifact when a case exposes a real defect, then deliver the complete revised draft and its unresolved adoption conditions.

Action-specific authority matters when composing permissions: separately valid fragments must actually cover the proposed combined action. A legitimate composite procedure can assemble complete grants; unrelated permissions cannot be stitched together to create a power none confers (§14.6). When authorized execution is in scope, continue through [create](create.md) and the host's controls, then verify the requested endpoint. An analysis can recommend without execution permission; a saved draft and an enacted policy are different deliverables.

## Worked route: redistribute recurring coordination work

Suppose one team member performs most visible tasks and also schedules, reminds, checks, and remains on call. First distinguish task hours from coordination labor and, where the work requires it, predictable uninterrupted time. Do not sum a continuous-time constraint with clock hours as if they were one measure. Compare relevant options such as rotating coordination, protected focus blocks, dedicated support, and reduced workload, including each option's costs to other positions.

A useful recommendation might adopt a time-limited rotation with training and protected support, under the disclosed aim of reducing concentrated burden without depriving anyone of necessary assistance. The deliverable is the actual rota, responsibilities, support commitment, review date, and correction channel. Track whether repeated allocation changes practice and capability before asserting a self-reinforcing capability loop; do not infer malice or permanent incapacity. An objection can warrant a revised schedule, a reasoned unchanged decision, or a protection response depending on what it establishes (§7.3.5, §7.3.9, §7.6.1).

## Composition and completion

Use [understand](understand.md) for unsettled factual or interpretive claims, [project](project.md) for consequences, [discover](discover.md) to generate omitted options, [create](create.md) for the actual artifact or authorized implementation, and [review](review.md) for challenge and correction. Pass the relevant premises, comparison, protected positions, remaining claims, and actual permission state; do not force every path through a choice form. Preserve this state proportionately under [continuity](../references/continuity.md).
