# Domain Atlas

An English distillation of the source's Appendix D (thirty-two domains). Each entry keeps the field's own question first, then names the distinctions that field already draws and the shortcut the frame must not take. Section anchors (§) point to the source body. The atlas is a retrieval aid: it does not summarize the cited literatures, does not rank traditions, and does not replace professional judgment, legal procedure, or the decision rights of the people concerned.

## How to Use the Atlas

1. Ask which result needs explaining before choosing vocabulary. Explaining why a device failed, why a decision formed, who bears the consequences, and how a person understands the experience are neighboring but different tasks.
2. Open the matching entry and keep that field's terms, units, measurements, identification conditions, and professional boundaries. When the field's own method answers the question adequately, the frame adds only source and boundary checks and opens no new circle.
3. Carry the listed distinctions into the object contract as separate variables or separate claims. Where an entry warns that two quantities differ, never let one stand in for the other in a later module.
4. An analysis can reach a conceptual answer, a textual answer, a formal answer, a domain-empirical answer, and a use-effect answer in turn. The first four do not vanish because the last one is missing, and clearer wording or more material never substitutes for observed use benefit (D.01).

Domains do not verify one another. Several studies that cite the same original work, data, or case remain one linked evidence chain, not independent confirmation.

## Shared Distinctions

These separations recur across many entries; apply them wherever the entry is silent.

- **Different proof duties.** Experience, reasoning, practice, embodied experience, textual interpretation, and normative argument each carry their own burden and are not pressed into one predictive score (D.02).
- **Design before name.** Invitation effects, effects on actual participants, whole-policy effects, and one person's outcome are different targets even under one program name (D.03).
- **Measurement and record formation.** Object, indicator, construct, and classification rule differ; reflective and formative indicators need different validity arguments; a falling count of positive records may come from lower risk, responses that avoided the outcome, or lower detection (D.03).
- **Joint feasibility.** One-at-a-time feasibility is not joint feasibility; formal feasibility, an existing algorithm, computability within budget, timely data, correct execution, and the right to adopt are six separate judgments (D.04, §2.1.2).
- **Value is not mechanism.** Stability, efficiency, legitimacy, and being worth maintaining can separate; resilience does not justify a system, and recovering the original state is not the only goal (D.04, D.19).
- **Pathways stay separate.** Formation, maintenance, function, social recognition, legal validity, and normative legitimacy answer different questions (D.14).
- **No universal conservation for social quantities.** Burden, suffering, attention, and moral responsibility have no conservation law; better technology can lower a cost without that cost having moved elsewhere (D.05).
- **Expression before vocabulary.** Expression, objection, and appeal must not wait until a person has learned the framework's terms (D.16, D.25).

## Foundations: D.01-D.05

### D.01 The framework itself, explanatory tasks, and theory borrowing

Anchors: §1.3-1.10, §2.2-2.7, §16.15-16.16. Borrowed theory is sorted into observational heuristic, definition, testable mechanism, formal consequence, or normative choice; the same word in two theories does not guarantee the same object, variables, or permitted inferences. A formal mapping says which relation it keeps, which distinction it loses, and what would change the conclusion. Trap: an automatic reminder reduced missed filings while appeals still cannot correct errors; "the system is better" hides the split, and calling the whole result void because one part failed is equally wrong. Historical adoption, mere mention, later-found compatibility, and the analyst's new connection stay separate; passing tests, complete files, and closed numbering prove engineering facts, not semantic correctness or real gain.

### D.02 Knowledge, concepts, context, and epistemic position

Anchors: §2.1-2.7, §4.8, §16.6, §16.15. A local counterexample may refute an auxiliary assumption or require a new object or measure; changing the question can open valuable research but cannot be back-filled as success on the old test. Useful, actionable, agreed, and true are different standards. Speech can describe or constitute action: promising, naming, granting standing, and reporting facts need different positions, rules, and evidence, and "I agree" may not amount to valid authorization under pressure. Epistemic position shapes who can reach material and who is believed; an excluded speaker's claims are reviewed on their content, neither disqualified in advance nor granted automatic truth.

### D.03 Research design, measurement, causality, and material formation

Anchors: §2.4-2.5, §4.1-4.6, §5.5.1, §12.3-12.7. Experiments, natural experiments, observational studies, process tracing, comparative history, oral, and archaeological material each solve part of identification. A total effect does not require every mediator to be identified, and a mechanism story does not guarantee a total effect. Controlling for a current state may remove a common cause, control away a historical channel, or open selection bias; when history acts through a current state, report total effect and conditional residual separately rather than calling history inert. Trap: approval rates rose after an application was simplified; whether livelihoods improved also needs actual payments, service quality, and the people who never applied. Archival silence counts against a claim only when such records would have been stably made, kept, and reachable.

### D.04 Systems, control, feedback, resilience, and delay

Anchors: §3.3, §4.5-4.6, §5.5, §6, §12.6. Observability and controllability are task-relative: a target quantity can be identified without reconstructing the whole state, and a rank condition on a control model is not executability under real deadlines, input limits, resources, and authority. A falling backlog asks whether demand fell, capacity rose, the counting boundary moved, or work was pushed elsewhere. Delayed feedback can destabilize even when it is effective.

### D.05 Information, representation, mathematics, and physics borrowing

Anchors: §2.6, §2.8, §4.6, §4.8, §5, Appendix C. A representation is judged on five dimensions: whether the source state can be recovered, whether the stated task can be completed, computational cost, whether future dynamics close, and whether closure survives allowed interventions. Unit conversion, accounting identity, empirical conversion rate, and value weight are separate kinds of equivalence. Incompleteness theorems do not imply that every finite task is undecidable. Model parsimony is judged on the whole selection process, including the tuning budget, not on the final formula alone. Social translation loss is never read as physical entropy.

## Human Action and Experience: D.06-D.10

### D.06 Human nature, evolution, habit, and capacity to act

Anchors: §7, §8.1, §9, §14.2-14.3. Current function, historical adaptation, drift, cultural transmission, personal intention, and welfare consequence answer different questions; overlapping explained variance is not first assigned wholly to one side. Treatment differences are explained by the full condition package. A constrained person really acting, being able to refuse safely, giving valid consent, and bearing a given responsibility are four judgments.

### D.07 Bounded rationality, emotion, group decision, and probing

Anchors: §9, §11.5-11.8, §12. Expert intuition can help where the environment has learnable regularities and valid feedback; seniority and confidence are not accuracy. Group judgment separates independent answering, independent relations, independent fact sources, and independent errors. Small probing steps have value under stated conditions. Sunk outlays differ from costs still to be paid; the evidential value of a message depends on its source structure and how it was selected for attention.

### D.08 Development, psychological experience, therapy theory, and professional boundaries

Anchors: §2.11, §9, §13.5, §16.12-16.14. A beneficial treatment package does not identify every mechanism its theory names; relaxation, relief, and narrative coherence do not certify the historical truth of a memory. The frame records only non-clinical states, context, privacy, and responsibility boundaries; needing support does not disqualify a person's results. Trap: stable housing made life more manageable while a symptom score did not change; the housing result stands, the clinical conclusion stays limited, and neither erases the other. Clinical and high-risk care judgments stay with professional processes, and professional standing never certifies a person's "essence".

### D.09 Love, care, recognition, meaning, and quality of life

Anchors: §1.8, §7.3.11, §9.7, §14.3, Appendix A.11. Love, care, and recognition do not merge into one functional variable. Private companionship, quiet watching, rest, hope, and mourning need not change group structure to be meaningful. X0 is not a template for human death or a universal grief sequence, and replaceable labor does not make a loved person replaceable. Trap: after leaving a relationship, direct contact may have to stop while dependent children still need safe continuity of care; continuity never becomes a reason to force renewed exposure, and a justified exit does not cancel bounded independent duties. Happiness, dignity, freedom from harm, and meaning can be weighed side by side without one total score imposed on the person.

### D.10 Ethical disagreement, capability, and finite responsibility

Anchors: §14, §15.2, §16.2. No single ethical theory is declared the winner across tasks. Resources become capabilities, choices, and outcomes only through conversion conditions; potential compensation is not actual compensation. In a tragic choice, writing down the unmet requirements and the remaining duties is more accountable than deleting the cost.

## Traditions and Culture: D.11-D.13

### D.11 Modern translation of Chinese thought and practice traditions

Anchors: §2.6-2.7, §9, §12, §14. Keep each text's historical conditions and internal tensions. Sincerity is not factual correctness. Others being willing to listen is not an institution making dissent safe enough to change a decision. Choosing equality and independent review in a modern translation is a present normative choice and is never written back as the full original meaning.

### D.12 Religion, civilizational comparison, and relational knowledge

Anchors: §2.7, §5.10, §7, §10, §16.15. A civilization is not a subject with one will. Knowledge being locatable, public, reusable, and authorized to represent its source community are different standings. A translation can suffice for one task while keeping unalignable parts for another; comparability across periods belongs to specific items and uses. Trap: a community's navigation practice reliably completes a class of tasks and an outside model explains part of the tracks; both can check their errors, the outside model gains no authority over life decisions because its equations are explicit, and the local method is not exempt from concrete safety checks because of cultural identity. Civilizational comparison is never written as a total ranking.

### D.13 Kinship, exchange, ritual, ethnography, and cultural change

Anchors: §2.5-2.7, §7.5, §9.7, §10, §16.6. Symbolic interpretation and material-institutional research correct each other without paying each other's debts. Ethnographic access, trust, recording, publication, and representation each need their own grounds. A source author's strong commitments stay as written, not softened into neutral reminders and then declared compatible. Trap: a festival adopts a new medium; participation and income change, some crafts survive and some experiences end; it can be genuine cultural creation with a clear loss, and "new creation" is neither forgery nor proof that internal control and distribution problems are absent.

## Society, Organization, and Economy: D.14-D.18

### D.14 Social theory, power, communication, and institutional reproduction

Anchors: §3.3, §7.6, §10-11, §14.5, §16.2. Public compliance has many causes. Participation may add expression without changing authority. A review that upholds a correct decision after real examination differs from one that upholds without review, and the two are distinguishable in reasons, material, and authority; a change rate cannot stand in for quality. Trap: an appeal heard and answered with reasons, decision upheld on sufficient evidence, shows that response happened, not that the request was adopted; an appeal that changed rules and resources does not by that change prove the distribution just.

### D.15 Games, cooperation, signals, commitment, and enforcement

Anchors: §10.5, §11.3, §11.7, §12, §14. The player set is not the whole set of representatives. Existence, selection, actual reach, dynamic stability, efficiency, and legitimacy of an equilibrium each need grounds. Commitment separates announcement, reason to perform, and ability to perform when due. A mechanism guarantee names its allowed action set; legal moves under the rules are not the moves actually available. Trap: a rule makes members report needs truthfully but hands monitoring to those who gain from violations; reporting works while enforcement fails, so revise that action and incentive chain rather than declaring rational models useless. Simulated violations are never thereby permitted.

### D.16 Organization, work, leadership, and labor protection

Anchors: §6.3-6.5, §7.6, §8, §15.3.3. Organizational learning separates forming a plan, leaving a record, actually invoking it, lasting change, and outcomes. Leadership is located in agenda, resources, interpretation, authorization, and error correction. Labor is assessed on hours, pace, skill, income, health, hidden maintenance, and care. Trap: output recovered under a new manager while a few people keep working overtime to cover gaps; the output gain is real, while sustainability, distribution, and succession remain open. Exit, split, merger, and collective bargaining check contracts, knowledge, representation scope, execution, and anti-retaliation. Framework standing is never an entry gate for workers organizing or appealing.

### D.17 Production, exchange, property rights, institutions, and development

Anchors: §2.8, §10.6.1, §12.10, §14.4, §15.3.5. An accounting identity is not a causal mechanism; ownership title is not actual control; a transaction occurring is not a proportionate choice. Reform comparisons fix the baseline and the transition path. A proportionate economics comparison is not weakened first and then declared completed. Trap: a public service is counted at cost in output statistics while residents still struggle to use it; it is neither "uncounted because non-market" nor "adequate because counted". Unpaid labor is not zero cost; redistribution is tracked through legal eligibility, application, actual receipt, and life outcome.

### D.18 Money, credit, finance, and crisis

Anchors: §2.8, §6.6, §6.12-6.13, §10.6.2, §12. Register currency, maturity, seniority, collateral, entity, and valuation basis. Trap: collateral falls from 100 to 80 against a loan of 60 under a 70% maximum loan-to-value ratio, so the permitted balance is 56 and a margin call of 4 follows; that is not a realized loss of 4, and a fire sale at 45 opens a separate account of repayment and loss. Revaluation, margin calls, sales, price feedback, and credit contraction are explained link by link. Rescue limits, disbursements, ex ante risk, and final net cost are settled separately. Splicing data across old and new legal definitions can manufacture false change.

## Politics, Law, and Inequality: D.19-D.22

### D.19 Political thought, institutional visions, and normative disagreement

Anchors: §2.2, §14, §16.2, §16.15. Comparing political thought yields no overall ranking. The framework's internal `authorized` and `applied` states are internal commitments and never pose as external legal effect. Stability, efficiency, and worth maintaining can separate. An irreplaceable checkpoint can turn into control.

### D.20 State capacity, policy implementation, and governance

Anchors: §7.3.7-7.3.8, §10-11, §14, §15.3.5. State capacity is located in specific tasks, levels, and organizational resources. Each link of a policy chain (agenda, rule, eligibility, budget, frontline execution, notice and application, determination and payment, actual use, appeal and correction) has its own denominator and clock; ratios of eligible, applicant, approved, and actual recipients are never mixed. Autonomy is stated relative to whom and over what. Trap: random inspections made records more complete while relief for ordinary applicants stayed slow; oversight effect and case relief each hold within their own range.

### D.21 Law, authorization, procedure, and responsibility

Anchors: §5.5.5, §5.5.7, §11.3, §13.5, §14, §15.2. Separate valid legal source, case application, moral reason, actual authority, remedy procedure, and lived delivery. Consent, authorization, agency, representation, and standing to sue differ; being heard, procedural respect, evidential correction, a changed decision, compensation, and rule change happen on different clocks. Trap: annual income is accurate yet cannot fix income within each short benefit window; correct arithmetic does not cure a legally decisive gap in timing information. When reliable records were converted on the wrong clock, correct the faulty inference and the decisions derived from it without deleting the reliable material. Organizational conditions can explain harm without automatically excusing individuals, and individual error does not delete upstream design, resources, and management.

### D.22 Class, identity, inequality, and social exclusion

Anchors: §2.5, §5.5.1, §9.7, §10.5-10.8, §14.4-14.5. Income, wealth, cash buffers, control over production and management, eligibility, social relations, and recognized status are different resources. Intersectionality asks how several rule sets jointly exclude one position. A narrowing relative gap and a widening absolute gap can hold together. Trap: a benefit raised average regional income while people without documents were neither counted nor able to apply; the average stays true, the coverage claim narrows, and those not reached are listed separately. Time, travel, care, and documentation costs of poverty are concrete constraints, never conclusions about ability or character.

## Life Course, Education, and Communication: D.23-D.27

### D.23 Family, gender, body, dependence, and care

Anchors: §9.7-9.8, §10.5-10.8, §14.3, §15.3.1-15.3.2. Care burden includes primary care time, simultaneous tasks, planning, standby, sleep, and future opportunity. Intimate conflict, sustained control, and acute danger differ and are not all handled by communication or joint therapy. A child's expression, the weight given to it, consent for a matter, and final decision authority are separate. Trap: an assistive arrangement lets a dependent person take part in decisions while still needing daily help; dependence and autonomy coexist, and a helper's ability creates no unlimited duty. Medical treatment, life improvement, and administrative eligibility do not certify each other.

### D.24 Population, generations, life course, and lives across places

Anchors: §5.5.1, §5.5.4, §6.12, §9.2, §10.7, §15.2.5. A demographic identity is not a policy goal. Age, period, and cohort have a linear identification problem. Migration separates legal status, aspiration, actual feasibility, and coercion. Succession is not conferred by age, and the year of a demographic turning point depends on the question. Trap: membership count is unchanged while experienced members left, newcomers arrived, and care needs rose; reading "full continuity" from the headcount misses knowledge and burden. A shared population goal owns no one's body; having children, not having them, and being able to raise them safely are normative questions, never duties assigned to individuals by statistical need.

### D.25 Education, knowledge production, professions, and lifelong learning

Anchors: §1.8, §6.3, §13, §15.3.3, §16.16. Educational aims, accessible learning, actual knowledge and skill, test interpretation, credential granting, and later opportunity are separate. A grade correction is also checked for whether it reached downstream decisions in time. Trap: trainees gained a new skill but no matching local jobs exist; that neither proves no learning occurred nor licenses promising employment because training finished. Open reading, reuse permission, contribution, co-defining questions, and labor recognition are different entry points; missing citizen observation records may mean coverage gaps, not absent objects.

### D.26 Networks, media, language, narrative, and identity

Anchors: §5.5.3, §5.10, §9.7, §10-11, §15.3.4, §15.3.6. Separate connection, actual help, exposure, reading, belief, public expression, and action. Simple contagion differs from complex contagion, which needs support from two different neighbors. Trap: two fully connected triads joined by one edge; if one acting neighbor suffices, adoption can cross the bridge; if two distinct neighbors are required, arrival of the message is not enough. The cumulative success of repeated independent tries, 1-(1-p)^n, is not social reinforcement, and several social nodes can still share one fact source. Unverified, proven false, and deliberately deceptive claims differ; facts learned later are never written back as known to the speaker on the day.

### D.27 Aesthetics, art, literature, games, and everyday culture

Anchors: §1.8-1.10, §5.10, §9.7, §10.6, Appendix A.3, A.11. Beauty, liking, artistic identity, use, popularity, income, grounds for public funding, and social effect differ. Play can be worthwhile for its process. "Function determines the score", "function suffices for beauty", and "this aspect has functional beauty" are different propositions.

## Technology, Space, Environment, Risk, and History: D.28-D.32

### D.28 Technology, infrastructure, platforms, and artificial intelligence

Anchors: §10-11, §13.8-13.11, §15.3.3-15.3.5, §16.23-16.24. Material properties, protocols, deployment configuration, institutions, maintenance, and actual use jointly determine outcomes; conformance to a standard text, interoperability, shared business semantics, reliability under stress, and real usability are judged layer by layer. For AI output, separate generation attribution, support in the original text, external facts, and fitness for use; separate suggestion, tool request, program execution, external state change, and consequence. Trap: a user clicks "decline" while a background task has already become irreversible; nominal approval is not a real veto. Privacy separates collection, inference, reuse, retention, and downstream deletion; issuing a deletion command does not prove completion. Data holding, permitted use, accounting recognition, trade, and cross-border provision are separate standings. Comparing technical necessity names whether the product name, binary, configuration, or full deployment is held fixed; limited reanalysis or synthetic recomputation is never reported as real users having improved. Before removing an old support that still carries a necessary function, check the replacement's availability and the consequences of interruption.

### D.29 Cities, space, housing, rural life, and public access

Anchors: §5.5.1-5.5.3, §10, §15.3.7-15.3.8. Geographic location, administrative jurisdiction, membership, functional network, and service area are different boundaries. Spatial visibility, lawful use, affordable continued access, safe autonomous movement, and power to change management rules are observed separately. Rural places are not a backward stage of cities. Trap: average income in a renewed neighborhood rose but the residents changed; the area-level change holds and cannot substitute for the life outcomes of original and displaced residents. After disasters, track building safety and re-exposure, household services, relationship networks, local memory, feasible choices, and remedies; relocation is not necessarily bad and rebuilding in place not necessarily good.

### D.30 Ecology, resources, commons, and sustainable living

Anchors: §2.8, §5, §6.9, §12, §14. Food stocks, supplies an organization can requisition, and food households actually obtain are three different quantities (Sen 1981). Common-pool resources separate access, withdrawal, management, exclusion, and transfer rights. Water withdrawal, net consumption, evapotranspiration, return flow, recharge, and water level differ; higher field efficiency followed by expanded acreage can save water locally without reducing total net use. Climate questions separate historical contribution, present capacity, future risk, and adaptation need; scientific findings do not by themselves set compensation shares or disposal power.

### D.31 Risk, disaster, public health, command, and recovery

Anchors: §6, §8.6, §12.6, §15.3.8, §16.13. Hazard, exposure, vulnerability, observation, risk acceptance, and action differ. Precaution weighs the action's own harms, proportionality, feasible support, and re-review. Critical slowing down is discussed only under its conditions. A warning counts only when it reaches an endpoint that can understand it, dispatch, and finish protection in time. Infection, testing, confirmation, reporting, and hospitalization have different denominators.

### D.32 War, revolution, empire, development, and historical paths

Anchors: §2.4-2.7, §8.1, §10-12, §14, §16.4. Identify the state as legal person, the government, the military, organizations, and the populations bearing consequences separately. Mobilization separates first participation, repeat participation, sustained organization, concessions won, transfer of power, and later life. Historical-dynamics models are not merged into one civilizational cycle. Diminishing marginal returns are not negative returns and not imminent collapse. Peace separates the end of violence, dispute procedures, political arrangements, accountability, restored lives, and reconciliation. Historical quantity comparisons first check targets and common support, never splicing intervals from different studies.

## What the Atlas Never Licenses

- Ranking domains, traditions, or civilizations against one another.
- Treating a domain's mention in the atlas as evidence that the framework's G, C, or CM claims hold there.
- Overriding a field's professional judgment, a court's or regulator's procedure, or the decision rights of the people concerned.
- Requiring anyone to translate their experience into atlas terms before it is acknowledged.
